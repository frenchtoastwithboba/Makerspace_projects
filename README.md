
# Makerspace_projects

#Clamp
I chose to design this clamp because I needed somewhere to put my viola bow down while practicing. If I needed to take notes, I had to walk to my desk and put my bow down. I found this quite annoying and time-consuming just to write a small note on my music. So, I designed a clamp that could attach to my stand and hold my bow.

I started by looking at a real metal clamp and studying its mechanism. Then I made a small model with VEX parts. Then I remade the model in TinkerCad and changed it to fit my needs. Once the clamp was able to grab onto objects, I redesigned it so it could be adjusted to different-sized objects.

From this project, I learned how to use basic CAD software. Starting from a simple model and refining it is a process that can lead to effective products. I learned how to implement holes for other parts, such as screws, and how to design them so they aren't too tight.

#Laptop Shelf
I designed a laptop shelf because I wanted to be able to free up space on my desk. I started off by figuring out materials and what I need. I chose to use wood to make the shelf and mount it onto a VESA monitor arm. I tried using wood from an old, used shelf, but the wood was hollow, wasn't genuine wood, and was covered in a plastic cover. When I glued it together, the pieces weren't strong enough to hold. So, I instead used actual wood and cut it using power tools. I glued them together with wood glue and sanded the shelf to make it look nice.

From designing the shelf, I learned the importance of material choice when making a product. Certain materials may not be suitable for certain tasks, such as the wood I used from existing furniture. When designing a product, engineers have to really consider what materials are capable of making the design work and succeed. An idea I have for the future is drilling holes in the shelf for better cooling of the laptop.
